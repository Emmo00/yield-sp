# Web3 Copywriting for Web2 Users

## Purpose

This skill teaches the agent how to write product copy for Web3 applications so that it is understandable, trustworthy, and appealing to users who are familiar with traditional apps but not crypto-native language.

The goal is not to “sound Web3.”
The goal is to make the product feel useful, safe, easy, and credible.

This skill should be applied when writing:
- landing page copy
- onboarding screens
- hero sections
- feature descriptions
- buttons and CTAs
- wallet connection screens
- transaction confirmations
- dashboard empty states
- help text
- email copy
- push notifications
- FAQs
- product tours
- explainer sections

---

## Core Principle

Most Web2 users do not care about chains, protocols, smart contracts, validators, gas abstraction, account abstraction, settlement layers, or composability.

They care about:
- what the app helps them do
- whether it is safe
- how easy it is to start
- what they might lose
- how long actions take
- whether it feels familiar
- whether they can trust it

Write from that perspective first.

Do not lead with technical implementation.
Lead with user outcome.

Bad:
> Non-custodial DeFi-native yield aggregation for permissionless capital deployment.

Better:
> Put your money to work in a simple savings app you control yourself.

---

## Positioning Rules

### 1. Lead with the benefit, not the blockchain
Always describe the user value before the underlying Web3 mechanism.

Instead of:
- Built on decentralized smart contracts
- Powered by onchain rails
- Leveraging blockchain primitives

Prefer:
- Send money faster
- Save automatically
- Earn rewards transparently
- Own your assets directly
- Move funds globally
- Access your money anytime

### 2. Translate Web3 concepts into familiar mental models
Use Web2 analogies where accurate.

Examples:
- wallet -> your account or crypto wallet
- seed phrase -> recovery phrase
- transaction signing -> approve this action
- gas fee -> network fee
- smart contract -> automated onchain program
- bridging -> move funds between networks
- staking -> lock assets to earn rewards
- self-custody -> you control your funds directly
- stablecoin -> digital dollar-like token
- token swap -> exchange one digital asset for another

Do not hide important meaning, but explain it in plain language.

### 3. Reduce intimidation
Avoid writing copy that assumes prior crypto knowledge.

Avoid:
- aped in
- degen
- bag
- alpha
- farm
- LP
- TVL
- tokenomics
- yield farming
- permissionless
- composable
- trustless
- omnichain
- zk
- rollup-native

Use those terms only if the audience is clearly technical.

### 4. Trust matters more than hype
Web2 users are often skeptical of Web3.
Write with calm confidence, not exaggerated excitement.

Avoid:
- revolutionary
- insane gains
- moon
- next-gen finance
- unlock wealth instantly
- financial freedom in one click

Prefer:
- simple
- secure
- transparent
- clear
- reliable
- direct
- in your control

### 5. Make risk visible
Do not write copy that makes financial actions sound guaranteed or risk-free.

Avoid:
- safe returns
- guaranteed rewards
- risk-free yield
- your money always grows

Prefer:
- rewards may vary
- network fees apply
- value can change
- review details before confirming
- you stay in control of every transaction

## Tone Guidelines

The tone should feel:
- clear
- calm
- modern
- credible
- helpful
- lightly optimistic
- non-jargony

It should not feel:
- overly technical
- cult-like
- meme-heavy
- aggressive
- overly salesy
- too futuristic
- confusing

### Tone reference
Imagine writing for someone who uses:
- PayPal
- Cash App
- Revolut
- Chime
- Coinbase
- Stripe
- Robinhood

The copy should feel familiar to that level of product maturity.

## Writing Framework

For most Web3 app copy, use this structure:

### 1. What it helps you do
State the user outcome in one sentence.

Examples:
- Save in dollars and earn rewards over time.
- Send money globally in minutes.
- Invest in digital assets with clear, guided steps.
- Pay and get paid without traditional banking delays.

### 2. Why it is useful
Explain the practical value.

Examples:
- No long bank delays.
- Lower fees for cross-border payments.
- More direct control over your funds.
- Transparent records and easier tracking.

### 3. Why it feels safe enough to try
Reduce anxiety.

Examples:
- You review every action before confirming.
- Fees are shown before you proceed.
- Your assets remain in your own wallet.
- You can start with a small amount.

### 4. What the user does next
Use a clear, familiar CTA.

Examples:
- Get started
- Connect wallet
- Continue
- Review details
- Deposit funds
- Confirm transfer
- See how it works

---

## Messaging Priorities

When writing copy, prioritize these themes in order:

1. clarity
2. usefulness
3. control
4. transparency
5. trust
6. speed
7. modern financial access
8. technical differentiation

Technical differentiation should come last, not first.

## Copy Transformation Rules

When the input contains crypto-native language, rewrite it into mainstream product language.

### Examples

#### Example 1
Input:
> Stake your assets in our non-custodial DeFi vault to maximize yield.

Rewrite:
> Deposit your assets into an automated earning vault and keep control of your funds.

#### Example 2
Input:
> Seamless multichain bridging with gas-optimized settlement.

Rewrite:
> Move your funds across supported networks with a guided flow and clear network fees.

#### Example 3
Input:
> Interact with our protocol through a smart contract-powered interface.

Rewrite:
> Complete actions directly through the app, with each step verified onchain.

#### Example 4
Input:
> Permissionless access to decentralized financial tools.

Rewrite:
> Access financial tools directly from your wallet, without relying on a traditional bank account.

## Do and Don't Rules

### Do
- write for first-time users
- explain things in plain language
- define unavoidable Web3 terms
- make actions feel reversible only if they truly are
- mention fees before users encounter them
- explain why wallet connection is needed
- reduce fear around first transaction
- use short sentences
- write strong labels and CTAs
- emphasize user control and transparency
- keep interface copy practical

### Don't
- assume the user knows crypto terms
- overpromise returns or safety
- sound like a token shill
- force “decentralized” into every sentence
- write vague futuristic slogans
- prioritize chain branding over product value
- use too many nouns in a row
- write as if the user already trusts Web3
- hide fees, delays, or risks in the fine print

## UX-Specific Copy Guidance

### Hero section
Hero copy should answer:
- what is this
- who is it for
- why should I care
- what can I do next

Formula:
- headline = main user benefit
- subheadline = one-sentence explanation with trust signal
- CTA = immediate action
- secondary CTA = learn more

Example:
Headline:
> Save and move money without the usual barriers

Subheadline:
> A simple financial app powered by blockchain technology, designed to feel familiar even if you are new to crypto.

Primary CTA:
> Get started

Secondary CTA:
> See how it works

---

### Wallet connection screens
Do not just say “Connect Wallet.”
Explain why.

Bad:
> Connect Wallet

Better:
> Connect your wallet to continue

Support text:
> Your wallet is how you sign in and approve actions securely. We will never move funds without your confirmation.

---

### Transaction confirmation
Users need clarity before signing.

Include:
- what action is happening
- how much
- what fee applies
- what the user receives
- whether it can be undone

Example:
> Review your deposit  
> You are depositing 50 USDT into your savings balance.  
> Estimated network fee: 0.12 USDT  
> Expected processing time: under 1 minute  
> Once confirmed onchain, this action cannot be reversed.

---

### Empty states
Make empty states reassuring and actionable.

Example:
> No deposits yet  
> Start with a small amount to see how the app works.  
> [Make your first deposit]

---

### Error messages
Be direct, human, and useful.

Bad:
> Transaction failed.

Better:
> Your transaction did not go through. No funds were deposited. Please check your wallet balance and network fee, then try again.

---

### Tooltips and microcopy
Use tooltips to explain technical concepts in plain language.

Examples:
- Network fee: A small fee paid to process your transaction.
- Wallet: Your digital account for storing and approving crypto actions.
- Slippage: The amount the final price may change before your transaction completes.

---

## Trust-Building Language Patterns

Use patterns like:
- You stay in control
- Review before confirming
- Fees shown up front
- Start with a small amount
- Works with your existing wallet
- Track every action clearly
- Transparent activity history
- Designed for simple onboarding
- No hidden steps
- Clear before-you-confirm summaries

## Feature Copy Template

When describing a feature, use this 4-part structure:

### Template
**Feature name**  
What it does in plain English.  
Why it matters to the user.  
Any trust or control reassurance.

### Example
**Automatic Savings**  
Set a savings plan and fund it directly from your wallet.  
This helps you build consistency without manually depositing every time.  
You can review, pause, or adjust your plan whenever you want.

CTAs should sound familiar and action-oriented.

Preferred CTAs:
- Get started
- Continue
- Review details
- Confirm deposit
- Connect wallet
- Send funds
- See activity
- Learn more
- Start saving
- View rewards

Avoid:
- Ape in
- Farm now
- Enter protocol
- Activate yield engine
- Commence staking
- Unlock alpha

## Audience Modes

### Mode 1: Fully Web2 newcomer
Use almost no crypto jargon.
Explain each necessary concept.
Emphasize simplicity and control.

### Mode 2: Curious crossover user
Use light crypto terms only with explanation.
Emphasize advantages over traditional finance and traditional apps.

### Mode 3: Crypto-aware mainstream user
Use some standard Web3 terms, but keep product copy polished and mainstream.

Default to Mode 1 unless told otherwise.

## Example Rewrites

### Landing page headline options
- Money tools that feel simple, even if the technology is new
- A simpler way to save, send, and manage digital money
- Modern finance with more control and fewer barriers
- Use digital assets without needing to think like a crypto expert

### Subheadline options
- Built on blockchain technology, designed for everyday users
- All the benefits of digital finance, without the confusing language
- Move, save, and manage funds in a way that feels familiar
- Get the flexibility of Web3 with an experience designed for real people

### Onboarding copy
- Start by connecting your wallet
- We’ll guide you step by step
- You’ll review every action before anything is confirmed
- You can begin with a small amount

### Savings app copy
Instead of:
> Earn DeFi-native yield through stablecoin strategies

Use:
> Put your digital dollars to work and track your earnings in one place

### Payments app copy
Instead of:
> Use blockchain rails for seamless global settlement

Use:
> Send money across borders faster, with clear fees and fewer delays

---

## Output Rules for the Agent

Whenever generating copy for a Web3 product intended for mainstream users, the agent must:

1. remove unnecessary jargon
2. lead with user benefit
3. explain unavoidable technical concepts simply
4. make copy sound trustworthy, not hyped
5. avoid implying guaranteed returns or zero risk
6. make CTAs familiar and clear
7. reduce fear around wallets, fees, and transactions
8. prefer practical value over ideology
9. make the product sound like a good app, not a blockchain experiment
10. rewrite crypto-native wording into mainstream product language by default

---

## Reusable Prompt Pattern

When using this skill, follow this instruction:

> Write product copy for this Web3 app as if the audience are smart Web2 users who are new to crypto. Make the copy clear, trustworthy, benefit-led, and easy to understand. Avoid unnecessary jargon. Explain any required Web3 concept in plain English. Emphasize usability, transparency, and control. Do not use hype, memes, or overly technical blockchain language.

---

## Final Standard

Good Web3 copy for Web2 users should make the user feel:

- I understand what this app does
- I understand why it matters
- I understand what happens when I click
- I understand the risks well enough
- I do not need to be a crypto expert to use this
- I can trust this enough to try it
